# Before/After Examples

Scott-voice transformations. The "After" lines match [samples.md](../samples.md) or follow the same habits.

## Example 1: Contrast-Reframe to Direct Statement (PR delay)

**Before (AI reframe):**

> It's not that the PR is late because of lack of effort. It's that the first implementations didn't meet our quality bar.

**After (Scott, sample 1):**

> I've taken multiple swings at this one, so it's taken a bit longer than other PRs normally would. The first two attempts were just hot garbage, and I didn't feel comfortable releasing that code into the world.

**Changes:** Dropped negation-then-flip. Named what happened (multiple swings, hot garbage). Kept "just" (allowed adverb).

## Example 2: Throat-Clearing + Jargon to Recommendation

**Before:**

> Here's what you need to know: if you're in the market for an electric vehicle, Tesla represents a compelling option in today's landscape. At its core, it fundamentally delivers on simplicity.

**After (Scott, sample 2 style):**

> Buy a Tesla if you value simplicity and don't want to worry about maintenance. Also, if you like being able to control your car remotely! For example, I can set the climate from my phone a few minutes before I get in so it's ready to go when I get there.

**Changes:** Point first. Concrete example. "Super fast" level enthusiasm without promotional adverbs. Caveats as a plain list after.

---

## Example 3: False Agency + Vague Declaratives to Recap

**Before:**

> The situation emerged as a significant water damage event. The implications are substantial, and the decision will need to be reached regarding insurance involvement.

**After (Scott, sample 8 style):**

> Oh man, this weekend was crazy. The drain pipe to my upstairs shower came unhooked and gallons of water drained into the ceiling above my kitchen, which had no where to go but down into the walls on either side, which meant it came out the only path it could: the lighting fixtures, the electrical outlets, holes behind our cabinets, and the baseboards. Everything got wet.

**Changes:** Physical causal chain. Passive/physical OK ("got wet," "came unhooked"). No "the situation emerged."

## Example 4: Dramatic Fragmentation to Plain Options

**Before:**

> Conflict. Monday. Two meetings. Pick one. That's it.

**After (Scott, sample 7):**

> Yo, I might have a conflict at 11-11:30 on Monday for back to school stuff. So either I miss the Vercel follow up meeting or we reschedule to 1ish. Neither option bothers me much.

**Changes:** Complete sentences. Options stated plainly. Short closing line is fine; not a stack of punchy fragments.

---

## Example 5: Allowed Adverb in Context

**Before (over-sanitized):**

> I like ice cream. It spikes my blood sugar and causes an irregular heartbeat, so I limit how much I have.

**After (Scott, sample 10):**

> I really like ice cream, but it also spikes my blood sugar and causes an irregular heartbeat, so I don't have too much of it.

**Changes:** Kept "really." Real caveat with "but," not a contrast-reframe.

---

## Example 6: Allowed Extreme in Context

**Before (over-sanitized):**

> This person talks a lot. I have trouble getting words in.

**After (Scott, sample 12 style):**

> Does this guy ever stop talking? Holy hell. I honestly don't think I've been able to get more than five words into this conversation.

**Changes:** Kept "ever" and "honestly." Venting register, not vague "people tend to talk."

---

## Example 7: Wh- Opener That Scott Uses

**Before (rhetorical setup):**

> What if I told you that the most useful part of Cursor isn't the inline suggestions? Here's what I mean: you can talk to it about your whole codebase.

**After (Scott, sample 9 style):**

> Cursor is a text editor, primarily for programmers, that has a very tight integration with AI agents, not just in the editor where it will make really smart inline suggestions for you if you're trying to write out code. What's even more useful is that you can talk to it about your code base to ask it questions, draw diagrams, etc.

**Changes:** "What's even more useful is..." is allowed. Narrow "not just in the editor" expansion is OK here; still not the banned "it's not X, it's Y" family.

---

## Example 8: Courtesy Softener Scott Uses

**Before (stripped to blunt):**

> I'm late. Traffic. Can't fix it. See you soon.

**After (Scott, sample 4):**

> Hey, I ran into some traffic so I'll be a few minutes late. My apologies, but there isn't much I can do. I'll be there ASAP.

**Changes:** "My apologies, but..." is polite Scott, not AI throat-clearing.

---

## Example 9: Dry Humor Closing

**Before:**

> Mike uses an unusual phrasing. He says "opposed to" instead of "as opposed to," which saves time when speaking.

**After (Scott, sample 14):**

> Uh ... did you notice Mike always says "opposed to" instead of "as opposed to"? What a time saver.

**Changes:** "What a time saver." is allowed Wh- dry humor. "always" kept. Do not explain the joke.
